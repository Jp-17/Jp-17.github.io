About

I'm a PhD student in Computational Neuroscience at Tsinghua University, Beijing, supervised by Prof. Xiaoxuan Jia in the Neural Coding Lab. My work sits at the intersection of neuroscience and AI, to understand how intelligent systems (brains and models) represent, reuse, and generalize knowledge.

Currently I am working on Brain Foundation Model research, to build large-scale pre-trained models for neural spiking data, which can generalize across sessions, subjects, and brain regions. The goal is to understand neural encoding mechanisms across modalities, and to leverage the learned universal representations for brain decoding tasks such as reconstructing perceived images and videos from neural activity.

Previously, I explored startup opportunities in the area of real-time interactive digital humans.

2026

NeuroHorizon: Long-Horizon Forward Prediction of Neural Population Activity via Autoregressive Decoding with Hierarchical Memory

Submitted to NeurIPS 2026 · Under review

This work studies forward prediction of neural population activity, a core requirement for closed-loop brain-computer interfaces and a stringent test of whether learned neural dynamics remain stable when a model conditions on its own prior predictions.

We introduce NeuroHorizon, an encoder-decoder architecture that combines event-level spike tokenization with an autoregressive causal decoder for population-level firing-rate prediction. A hierarchical tail+segment memory preserves recent predictions at high resolution while compressing longer prediction history, and scheduled sampling reduces the mismatch between teacher-forced training and autoregressive rollout.

On a multi-horizon motor-cortex benchmark, with additional motor- and visual-cortex datasets for scaling and cross-population transfer, the model consistently outperforms strong baselines and remains stable under multi-step rollout where prior approaches collapse, supporting long-horizon neural forecasting for closed-loop BCI and multi-step neural-state prediction.
